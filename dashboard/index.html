    Critical roads with no sensor

      Where the next sensors should go

      Of all shortest routes between junctions, a few roads carry most of them. Those are the critical roads, the top tenth of road length by that measure. Most of them have no sensor.

      How much rain slows a free-flowing road

      Change in speed against each road's own dry-weather normal for that weekday and hour, across all three cities. Whiskers are 95% intervals.

      Filled dots: the interval stays below zero, a clear slowdown. Hollow dots: the interval crosses zero, so the change is within normal day-to-day variation.

      Why it flags slowdowns rather than forecasting them

      The same model, trained with and without rain data, predicting how far each reading strays from normal. Lower error is better.

      Built from

      Raw loop-detector readings, checked at every stage so no row is lost without a reason.

        Traffic: UTD19 loop-detector archive, ETH Zürich (Loder et al., 2019). Rain: ERA5 reanalysis, Copernicus Climate Change Service, downscaled to 2 km and 10 minutes with spateGAN-ERA5 (Glawion et al., 2025). Roads: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

        The 2 km rain is a model estimate, not a gauge measurement. Road importance is network structure (shortest-route share), not a traffic count. Map roads are current OpenStreetMap; traffic is from 2016–2017.
